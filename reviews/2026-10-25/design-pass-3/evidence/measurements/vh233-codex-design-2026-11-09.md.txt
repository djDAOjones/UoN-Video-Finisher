2. **My smallest candidate is one scoped offset override**, beside `.statement-body` in `src/styles/app.css`:

   ```css
   #accessibility-statement a:focus-visible {
     outline-offset: calc(var(--focus-offset) - var(--focus-width));
   }
   ```

   With `tokens.carbon.css:97–101`, this computes to **zero offset**, retaining the existing **2 px outline**. It brings each outline towards its fragment and should separate the adjoining paint without changing layout. This is a design candidate requiring the specified Chrome walk, not an experimentally established fix.

   The unfocused link’s colour, underline, wrapping, dimensions and target remain unchanged. The focused link retains its underline and colour too. No padding, display, line-height or wrapping rule changes. Although `test/stylesheet.test.ts` explicitly exempts structural zero, the expression also respects this brief’s stricter token-only wording.

   Crucially, this sets **only offset**: the global outline and forced-colours `Highlight` override remain effective. Dark and `.on-brand-blue` retain their existing white `--focus` (`tokens.brand.css:173,202`). The statement’s captured white-on-blue appearance remains. This preserves the colour mechanism supporting 2.4.7 and 1.4.11; their rendered visibility and adjacent-colour contrast must still be checked, particularly around glyphs and underlines.

   I reject inline-block as the first remedy because it changes sentence wrapping and the measured component; padding/margins alter layout; box-shadow is unreliable in forced colours; and increasing outline width can enlarge the same merged contour and encroach on neighbouring text.
